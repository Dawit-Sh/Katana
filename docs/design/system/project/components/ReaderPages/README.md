# Pages: turn or scroll

Every reader offers both ways of reading, as the first row of its settings (Katana, 2026-11-19): **Pages**, an `AppChoiceRow` with **Turn** and **Scroll** (`ReaderPagesMode`, label `ReaderPagesLabel`). Turn is one page, or a spread, at a time; Scroll is the book as one strip.

- **PDF and DjVu** get the reading settings card the comics reader has (the `tune` button at the end of the top bar, `settings-width` 400, a bottom sheet on phones): Pages, then Page number. Scrolling, the pages run top to bottom as wide as the view, 6 apart on `surface-container-high`; a page not drawn yet is a plain page-coloured box. Pinch zoom works (up to 4×); zoomed, a drag pans sideways as well as down the strip, and a double tap zooms to 2.5× and back. The page number, the slider and the page strip follow the page across the middle of the screen; a tap shows or hides the bars; a pull past the last page finishes the book, as Next on the last page does.
- **Comics:** Pages, then **Direction**: Left to right or Right to left when turning, and also **Top to bottom** (webtoons) when scrolling, where left and right run the strip sideways with each page shown whole. Direction is always stacked under its label, so the card keeps its height. Then the rows that only apply to turning pages, disabled while scrolling: **Two pages** (Auto, Off, On), Page turn, Fit. The slider fills the way the pages go.
- **EPUB** (and MOBI, AZW, AZW3, FB2): Layout's first row is Pages; then, where a spread fits (Readium needs a page area 960 wide), **Two pages** (Auto, Off, On, "Auto: two in landscape"), and Page turn, both disabled while scrolling. Scrolling is Readium's: each chapter scrolls, and a drag up that starts at a chapter's end goes on into the next (down at its top, back to the one before).
- **Page turn** (comics and EPUB): **Slide**, **Curl**, or **None**, where the page just changes with no animation, from a tap, a volume key or a swipe alike (a swipe still has to go sideways past the touch slop, and a drag up or down, a long press or a second finger stays with the page). PDF and DjVu turning has no animation of its own, so it has no Page turn row.
- **Kept per book**, in the book's reader session. A book not set yet: a PDF or DjVu book opens the way you last picked in any of them; an EPUB with the look "Use these for new books" saved (it now keeps Pages and Two pages too, and Settings › Default look for new books lists them); a comic with its library's reading defaults (Settings › a server › Reading defaults: Turn or Scroll), its series' choice, then its own. A comic's scroll direction is its own, top to bottom until picked.
- **The place carries over:** switching keeps the page you are on (scrolling, the page across the middle of the screen); a scrolling book reopens where it was left, down to the spot in the page, unless the saved page has moved since (another device, or turning pages).
- **Words in a PDF** (2026-11-20, Turn and Scroll alike): a long press selects the word under the finger (a vibration says so) and, without lifting, a drag adds whole words, across lines; then two handles (teardrops, radius 10, in the selection blue at full strength) move each end a character at a time. The selection fills with the EPUB selection blue (`ReaderSelectionHighlight`). Once the finger lifts, Android's own floating text toolbar offers **Highlight**, **Note** and **Copy**, as in an EPUB; Back or a tap on the page clears the selection. Highlights draw over the page image in their style and colour (a fill at the colour's 38%, a line under, a line through or a wave), exact at any zoom; a tap on one opens the same highlight card as an EPUB's. A scanned page has no text: a long press there does nothing. Words that run down, up or upside down (a page turned by its /Rotate, a table printed sideways) select the same way; the handles hang from the true start and end of the words, on the next line's side. **DjVu books** do all of this with their hidden text layer (the OCR text most scanned DjVu books carry), word boxes shared out among their letters; a DjVu page without the layer has nothing to select.
- **The PDF and DjVu top bar on a phone** (under 600 wide) holds what an EPUB's does: search, bookmark, marks and Aa after Back, so the title keeps its room. Previous and Next page join them on wider screens; on a phone taps, swipes, the volume keys and the slider turn pages. In every reader the title sits centred while that leaves it room, else between Back and the end's buttons; where not even a button's width is left (320 wide, the largest display size) it isn't drawn, so no lone "…" reads as a button.
